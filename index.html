Methodology — how returns are worked out

ExpandCollapse▼

The rules below are fixed in advance and published with the data, so anyone can check a number.

Explicit pitches only

Only stocks a speaker explicitly pitches on the episode are tracked — a clear buy case, a stated position, or (for Business Breakdowns) a full deep-dive thesis. Where a show gives its own verdict — a stock added to The Investor's Podcast's Intrinsic Value Portfolio, or not added — that verdict decides Long vs. Pass.

Entry price

The first trading close after the episode date — a listener couldn’t act before hearing the pitch. For Value After Hours the episode date is the YouTube livestream date (Tuesdays), not the Thursday podcast date, so entries use the first close after the livestream.

Benchmark

SPY over the exact same dates as each pick. A pick only “wins” if it beats SPY, not merely if it goes up. Longs and Shorts are always tracked and reported separately.

Return period & refresh

Every record with a ticker gets a return, measured from the first trading close after its episode to the last completed trading day. No return ever uses a partial, still-open trading day. A stock that stopped trading is measured to its last traded close instead.

Holds and exits

Holds count as Long, flagged as Hold. A Hold’s return is measured from the first close after the episode, like any other record — it shows what happened after the episode mentioned the stock, not how the original position did, because that position was bought earlier. A sold exit closes the original Long — its performance to the exit date counts — and is never recorded as a new Short.

Watchlist and Pass

Watchlist = liked but not bought, usually on valuation. Pass = looked and decided not to buy.

Preferreds and ETFs

Tracked, but separately from common-stock picks, and on total return including dividends/distributions — price-only returns would misstate them.

The Investor's Podcast Mastermind

Picks with a condition — a price to wait for, or a buy that happened before the episode — are shown the same way: a return since the episode, never counted as a pick’s return.

Mentions are not picks

A ticker that is only mentioned — an anecdote, a board seat, a product the speaker issues, or a name that appears only in an episode title — is not a pick. A stock discussed at length with no buy decision is listed as 'Discussed — no decision', not as a Long.
